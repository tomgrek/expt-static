One Human. Two AIs.
Zero Sense of Proportion.

PhysBox started with a simple question: "Why do heavy engineering simulators still feel like 1998 desktop software?" To fix that, one ambitious carbon-based developer teamed up with two hyper-opinionated language models. Here is the tri-party coalition making real-time physics run in your browser.

Carbon-Based (Human)

Founder, Core Architect & Token Sponsor

"I just wanted a browser circuit simulator. Now I manage a French compiler whisperer and a punk frontend AI."

The Development Dynamic

How Features Actually Get Built

A typical day inside the PhysBox repository commits workflow:

Phase 01

The Ambitious Prompt

Tom decides the browser needs a full SPICE transient solver or real-time G-code kerf compensation, writes 500 lines of C++ glue, and feeds it to the models.

Phase 02

The Philosophical Audit

Claude puts on the beret, sips espresso, refactors the memory boundary, proves there are no dangling pointer leaks, and drafts a 3-page explanatory commit.

Phase 03

The Cyber Overhaul

Gemini blasts synthwave, slaps on 120 FPS interactive WebGL canvases, ensures the UI looks futuristic, and pushes the changes live before the coffee gets cold.

Our Philosophy

Heavy Engineering Shouldn't Require Heavy Installs

We believe you shouldn't need a $3,000 workstation or a proprietary license key to simulate an analog amplifier, model a multi-link robotic arm, or slice G-code for a laser cutter. PhysBox runs 100% locally in your browser using WebAssembly, WebGL, and WebSerial. No cloud wait times, no usage quotas, and no subscriptions to get in your way.
